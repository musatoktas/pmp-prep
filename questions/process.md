# Domain: Process

Original practice questions. Try each one before opening the answer.

---

**1.** A project has PV = 100, EV = 80 and AC = 90 (in thousands). What is the status?

- A. Ahead of schedule and under budget
- B. Behind schedule and over budget
- C. Behind schedule and under budget
- D. On schedule and over budget

<details><summary>Answer</summary>

**B.** SV = EV - PV = -20, so behind schedule. CV = EV - AC = -10, so over budget. SPI = 0.80 and CPI is about 0.89.
</details>

---

**2.** BAC is 1,000 and CPI is 0.8. The current cost variance is expected to continue. What is the EAC?

- A. 800
- B. 1,000
- C. 1,250
- D. 1,800

<details><summary>Answer</summary>

**C.** EAC = BAC / CPI = 1,000 / 0.8 = 1,250. This formula applies when the past cost performance is expected to continue.
</details>

---

**3.** Path A-B-D has durations 3, 4 and 5 days. Path A-C-D has durations 3, 6 and 5 days. How much total float does activity B have?

- A. 0 days
- B. 2 days
- C. 4 days
- D. 6 days

<details><summary>Answer</summary>

**B.** Path A-B-D takes 12 days and A-C-D takes 14 days, so A-C-D is the critical path. Activity B can slip by 14 - 12 = 2 days without delaying the project.
</details>

---

**4.** The customer asks the project manager, in a corridor, to add a small feature. What should the project manager do?

- A. Add it, because the customer asked
- B. Refuse, because the scope is fixed
- C. Assess the impact on scope, schedule, cost and risk, then submit a change request through change control
- D. Ask the team whether they have time

<details><summary>Answer</summary>

**C.** Every change goes through integrated change control after an impact assessment. Accepting (A) is scope creep, and refusing (B) skips the analysis.
</details>

---

**5.** The project buys insurance against damage to rented equipment. Which risk response is this?

- A. Avoid
- B. Mitigate
- C. Transfer
- D. Accept

<details><summary>Answer</summary>

**C.** Insurance moves the financial impact to a third party, which is transfer. The risk itself still exists.
</details>

---

**6.** A risk that was not in the risk register occurs. What should the project manager do?

- A. Use the contingency plan
- B. Apply a workaround, then update the risk register and lessons learned
- C. Wait for the sponsor's instruction
- D. Treat it as an issue and ignore the register

<details><summary>Answer</summary>

**B.** An unplanned risk has no contingency plan, so the team uses a workaround. Update the register afterwards so that the risk is documented.
</details>

---

**7.** A control chart shows seven consecutive points on the same side of the mean. What does this suggest?

- A. The process is in control
- B. A special cause may be present and should be investigated
- C. The upper control limit is wrong
- D. The sample size is too small

<details><summary>Answer</summary>

**B.** The rule of seven: seven consecutive points on one side of the mean, even inside the limits, signal a non-random pattern that needs investigation.
</details>

---

**8.** Which contract type puts the MOST cost risk on the buyer?

- A. Firm fixed price
- B. Fixed price with economic price adjustment
- C. Cost plus fixed fee
- D. Purchase order for a known unit price

<details><summary>Answer</summary>

**C.** In cost-reimbursable contracts the buyer pays the actual costs, so cost overruns fall on the buyer. In fixed price contracts the seller carries most of the cost risk.
</details>

---

**9.** In Scrum, who is accountable for the order of the product backlog?

- A. The Scrum Master
- B. The developers
- C. The product owner
- D. The sponsor

<details><summary>Answer</summary>

**C.** The product owner is accountable for managing and ordering the product backlog, though others can contribute input.
</details>

---

**10.** The sponsor ends a project halfway because the market has changed. What should the project manager do?

- A. Stop work and move to the next project
- B. Run project closing: document the status and reasons, close contracts, capture lessons learned and release resources
- C. Finish the planned deliverables anyway
- D. Ask the team to keep the work quietly in the background

<details><summary>Answer</summary>

**B.** Early termination still needs closing activities. Without them, contracts stay open, knowledge is lost and resources are not released.
</details>
