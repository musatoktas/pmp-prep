# PMP Prep: an 8-week plan for the 2026 exam

An open study plan for the PMI **Project Management Professional (PMP)** exam. It is built for working professionals who can give the exam about **2 focused hours on weekdays and 4 hours on Saturday for 8 weeks**, roughly 120 hours in total, including the 35 hours of project management education that PMI requires before you apply.

I am using it for my own preparation, next to my CISA work (see [cisa-prep](https://github.com/musatoktas/cisa-prep)). I come from software delivery, ISO/IEC 27001 auditing and BSI training, so the plan leans on governance, risk and change control where it helps. It should work for anyone who has led projects.

> Not affiliated with or endorsed by PMI. PMP and PMBOK are registered marks of the Project Management Institute. All sample questions here are original and written for practice only. They are not taken from the exam or from any exam dump. Always check exam facts against the current PMP Exam Content Outline and the PMI Certification Handbook.

## What changed in July 2026

PMI updated the PMP exam on **9 July 2026**. Older books, videos and question banks can be out of date. Third-party sources describe the new exam as 180 questions in 240 minutes, with more case-based and graphic-based questions, and with risk, change control and governance topics moving into the Business Environment domain. The domain weights below come from training providers, not from PMI. **Replace them with the numbers in PMI's current Exam Content Outline before you rely on them.**

## The exam in one table

| Item | Detail (check the current PMI handbook) |
|---|---|
| Questions | 180, a mix of multiple choice and other formats |
| Time | 240 minutes (reported for the July 2026 exam) |
| Delivery | Test centre or online proctored |
| Approach | About half of the content is predictive, half agile or hybrid |
| Education before applying | 35 contact hours of project management education, or a CAPM certificate |
| Experience before applying | With a four-year degree, 36 months leading projects within the last 8 years. Without one, 60 months |
| Scoring | Reported by proficiency level per domain, not as one percentage |

## The weekday shape

| Block | Time | Activity |
|---|---|---|
| 1 | 40 min | **Read or watch**: the day's topic |
| 2 | 20 min | **Condense**: a one-page summary in your own words and 5 flashcards |
| 3 | 45 min | **Practise**: 25 to 30 timed questions on the day's topic |
| 4 | 15 min | **Review**: read every explanation and log every miss in the tracker |

Saturday is a 4-hour block with a mixed set of 60 questions and a full review. Sunday is rest, or 30 minutes with your wrong-answer log.

## How to think like a PMP

1. **Act, do not complain.** The project manager solves problems inside their authority. If a change is outside it, raise it through the right channel.
2. **Analyse before acting.** When a change, risk or conflict appears, assess the impact before you react.
3. **Talk first, escalate later.** Direct, private conversation comes before escalation and before formal discipline.
4. **Change goes through change control.** Even a small request from the sponsor needs an impact assessment and a decision by the right authority.
5. **Servant leadership in agile.** Remove impediments, protect the team, and let the team own how it works.
6. **Value and compliance.** Deliver value to the business, and never drop a legal or regulatory requirement to save time.
7. **Read the qualifier.** *First*, *best*, *most likely* and *next* change the answer. Several options are often true.

## Use AI to study, not to sit the exam

AI is a good tutor for plans, explanations and original practice scenarios. It is not allowed in the exam. PMI proctors the exam and treats unauthorised help as an ethics violation. Do not use real exam questions or dumps either. Prepare with your own knowledge so the certificate means something.

## Using this repository

1. Star or fork it, then copy [`tracker.md`](tracker.md) into your own notes.
2. Pick an authorised 35-hour course that covers the current exam, and fit it into weeks 1 to 4.
3. Follow [`plan/week-1.md`](plan/week-1.md).
4. Use the questions here as a warm-up. A paid simulator that matches the July 2026 exam should be your main question source.

Corrections and new original questions are welcome as pull requests.
