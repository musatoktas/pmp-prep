# Domain: People

Original practice questions. Try each one before opening the answer.

---

**1.** Two senior engineers disagree about the technical approach, and the argument is delaying the work. What should the project manager do FIRST?

- A. Escalate the conflict to the functional manager
- B. Choose the approach that is cheaper and tell both to follow it
- C. Bring them together to discuss the facts and look for a solution both accept
- D. Ask the team to vote on the approach

<details><summary>Answer</summary>

**C.** Collaborating (problem solving) is the preferred first response. Escalation (A) comes later, forcing (B) damages the relationship, and a vote (D) leaves one side as the loser without a shared understanding of the problem.
</details>

---

**2.** In an agile team, a developer cannot continue because another department has not approved access to a test environment. As servant leader, what should the project manager do?

- A. Tell the developer to take another item and wait
- B. Contact the other department and remove the impediment
- C. Ask the sponsor to reprioritise the whole backlog
- D. Record the delay and discuss it at the end of the sprint

<details><summary>Answer</summary>

**B.** A servant leader removes impediments quickly. Waiting (A, D) lets the delay grow, and involving the sponsor (C) is an escalation that is not needed yet.
</details>

---

**3.** A new project team has just been formed. Members are polite, unsure of their roles, and look to the project manager for guidance. What should the project manager do?

- A. Delegate most decisions to the team
- B. Give clear direction, define roles and agree ground rules
- C. Focus on resolving conflicts between members
- D. Reward the best performers publicly

<details><summary>Answer</summary>

**B.** This is the forming stage. People need direction, clear roles and shared rules. Delegation (A) fits later stages, and there are no conflicts to resolve yet (C).
</details>

---

**4.** A regulator has high power over the project but little interest in its day-to-day progress. How should the project manager engage this stakeholder?

- A. Monitor only
- B. Keep informed
- C. Keep satisfied
- D. Manage closely

<details><summary>Answer</summary>

**C.** High power with low interest calls for keeping the stakeholder satisfied without overloading them with detail. Manage closely is for high power and high interest.
</details>

---

**5.** A team member has missed three deadlines in a row. What should the project manager do FIRST?

- A. Give the work to someone else
- B. Report the member to the functional manager
- C. Speak privately with the member to understand the cause
- D. Add the member's tasks to the risk register

<details><summary>Answer</summary>

**C.** Understand the cause before acting. The reason may be workload, a skill gap or something outside the project. Escalation (B) and reassignment (A) come after the conversation.
</details>

---

**6.** A virtual team in four time zones keeps misreading each other's written messages. Which action is BEST?

- A. Ask everyone to join a daily call at the same time
- B. Agree communication norms in a team charter and use video for complex topics
- C. Move all discussions to a single long email thread
- D. Replace remote members with local staff

<details><summary>Answer</summary>

**B.** Working agreements and richer media for complex topics address the cause. A daily call across four time zones (A) is unfair, and a long email thread (C) makes misreading worse.
</details>

---

**7.** One senior member dominates every meeting while others stay silent. What should the project manager do?

- A. Ignore it, because the senior member has the best ideas
- B. Use structured facilitation such as round-robin input and give the senior member private feedback
- C. Remove the senior member from the meetings
- D. Ask the silent members to send ideas by email afterward

<details><summary>Answer</summary>

**B.** Facilitation draws in the quiet members, and private feedback deals with the behaviour without embarrassing anyone. Ignoring (A) loses ideas, and removing (C) is too harsh.
</details>

---

**8.** After a sprint demo failed, team members start blaming each other. What should the project manager do?

- A. Identify who caused the failure and give feedback
- B. Cancel the next sprint to calm things down
- C. Facilitate a retrospective that focuses on process and the system, not on people
- D. Replace the product owner

<details><summary>Answer</summary>

**C.** A blameless retrospective keeps psychological safety and finds causes in the process. Naming individuals (A) discourages honest reporting.
</details>

---

**9.** A self-organising team cannot agree on an estimate for a backlog item. What is the BEST approach?

- A. The project manager decides the number
- B. Take the average of the highest and lowest estimates without discussion
- C. Have the people with the extreme estimates explain their reasoning and estimate again
- D. Drop the item from the backlog

<details><summary>Answer</summary>

**C.** Differences often show hidden assumptions. Discussing and re-estimating (as in planning poker) uses the team's knowledge. The project manager should not impose a number on a self-organising team (A).
</details>

---

**10.** The sponsor cancels a feature the team worked on for a month, and morale drops. What should the project manager do?

- A. Tell the team that decisions are the sponsor's job
- B. Acknowledge the concern, explain the reason for the decision and the value of the work done
- C. Give the team a bonus
- D. Ask the sponsor to reverse the decision

<details><summary>Answer</summary>

**B.** Emotional intelligence: listen, acknowledge and give context. A dismissive reply (A) lowers morale further, and a bonus (C) or reversal (D) does not address the cause.
</details>
